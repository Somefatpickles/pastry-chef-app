# Household baking measurements

The ingredient checklist and AI proposal preview now show cups, tablespoons, teaspoons, counts, or familiar package ounces. All 694 ingredient lines across 68 library recipes were checked, including half and 2.5× batches. Exact canonical quantities remain unchanged for scaling, saved recipes and AI context. The display never treats every ingredient as if it had the same cup weight.

Dry equivalents depend on the ingredient and its preparation: flour is spooned and leveled, brown sugar packed, and fruit prepared as described in its measuring note. Mass-to-volume values are approximate. Precision-sensitive almond flour, gelatin, egg whites and lamination butter retain the original quantity in the measuring note. Blueberry cup weights vary materially; the displayed estimate uses the midpoint of the published range. Apples use loosely measured peeled slices, then further chopping as directed; pitted cherries use the published chopped-fruit equivalent. Mixed fruit, purée, praline paste and unspecified alternative mixtures retain ounces instead of assuming an unsupported density.

Cup fractions use familiar spoons for remainders. A common fraction is selected only within about 1.1% of the calculated display measure; otherwise the remainder is retained, including small decimal teaspoons when needed. Display rounding does not change the stored formula. Ounces are weight, not fluid ounces. Packaged pastry, chocolate bars, cream cheese and condensed milk are conveniently measured using package-weight markings.

Research, accessed October 24, 2026:

- [King Arthur Baking ingredient weight chart](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/ingredient-weight-chart): ingredient-specific dry cup weights, dairy, butter, fruit preparation, chocolate chips and nuts. Its recommendation to weigh for precision still applies even though household measures are the primary display.
- [NIST approximate kitchen equivalents](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/metric-si/metric-kitchen/metric-kitchen-cooking-measurement-equivalencies): the app's conventional baking equivalents use 240 ml per cup, 15 ml per tablespoon and 5 ml per teaspoon. These are approximate kitchen equivalents rather than a claim that a customary US cup is exactly 240 ml.
- [Red Star dry yeast label and preparation](https://redstaryeast.com/product/all-natural-active-dry-yeast-strip/): a 7-g packet corresponds to 2¼ teaspoons of dry yeast. [Fleischmann's](https://www.fleischmannsyeast.com/baking-basics/yeast-101/) independently specifies the same packet spoon measure.
- [Knox gelatin guidance](https://knoxgelatine.com/about-us): the powder spoon estimate follows its 7-g / 2½-teaspoon guidance. Gelatin brand and bloom strength still matter; the original weight remains available for precision.
- [Morton table-salt label](https://www.mortonsalt.com/shop/table-salt/morton-table-salt/): fine table salt's spoon conversion uses its labeled serving weight. This does not apply to kosher salt crystal sizes.
- [Crisco vegetable-oil label](https://crisco.com/products/pure-vegetable-oil/): oil mass display uses the manufacturer’s tablespoon serving weight.
- [McCormick ground-cinnamon product nutrition sheet](https://www.webstaurantstore.com/documents/nutrition/932463mccormickcinnamonground.pdf): level-spoon weight is brand-specific; the display marks this as approximate.

The changes supply familiar measuring guidance; they do not establish that any recipe has been physically baked or that a volume equivalent guarantees identical results across brands and packing styles.
